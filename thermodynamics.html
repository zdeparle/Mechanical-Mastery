🌡️ Thermodynamics

Heat, energy, work, and the laws governing energy transfer in engineering systems

Ideal Gas Law

$PV = nRT$
P — Pressure
Absolute pressure (Pa, kPa, atm)
V — Volume
Volume of gas (m³, L)
n — Moles
Amount of substance (mol)
R — Gas Constant
8.314 J/(mol·K)
T — Temperature
Absolute temperature (K)

First Law of Thermodynamics

$\Delta U = Q - W$
ΔU — Change in Internal Energy
Net energy change in the system (J, kJ)
Q — Heat Added
Heat transferred into system (J, kJ)
W — Work Done by System
Work performed by system on surroundings (J, kJ)

Heat Transfer

$Q = \frac{kA\Delta T}{L}$
Q — Heat Transfer Rate
Rate of heat conduction (W)
k — Thermal Conductivity
Material property (W/(m·K))
A — Cross-sectional Area
Area perpendicular to heat flow (m²)
ΔT — Temperature Difference
Difference across material (K, °C)
L — Thickness
Material thickness (m)
$Q = mc\Delta T$
Q — Heat Energy
Heat absorbed or released (J, kJ)
m — Mass
Mass of substance (kg)
c — Specific Heat Capacity
Heat per unit mass per degree (J/(kg·K))
ΔT — Temperature Change
Change in temperature (K, °C)

Efficiency & Cycles

$\eta_{Carnot} = 1 - \frac{T_C}{T_H}$
η — Carnot Efficiency
Maximum theoretical efficiency (dimensionless)
T_C — Cold Reservoir Temperature
Absolute temperature (K)
T_H — Hot Reservoir Temperature
Absolute temperature (K)
$\eta = \frac{W_{net}}{Q_H}$
η — Thermal Efficiency
Cycle efficiency (dimensionless)
W_net — Net Work Output
Net work produced (J, kJ)
Q_H — Heat Input
Heat absorbed from hot reservoir (J, kJ)
