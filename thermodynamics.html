🌡️ Thermodynamics

Understand heat, energy, and entropy in engineering systems

First Law of Thermodynamics

$\Delta U = Q - W$
ΔU - Change in Internal Energy
Change in internal energy of system (J, kJ, BTU)
Q - Heat Transfer
Heat added to system (J, kJ, BTU, cal)
W - Work Done
Work done by system (J, kJ, BTU)
$W = \int P \, dV$
W - Work
Work done (J, kJ, BTU)
P - Pressure
Pressure (Pa, kPa, MPa, psi, atm)
V - Volume
Volume (m³, L, ft³, in³)

Ideal Gas Law

$PV = nRT$
P - Pressure
Pressure (Pa, kPa, MPa, psi, atm)
V - Volume
Volume (m³, L, ft³, in³)
n - Number of Moles
Amount of substance (mol)
R - Gas Constant
Universal gas constant (8.314 J/(mol·K))
T - Temperature
Absolute temperature (K, °C, °F)

Heat Transfer

$Q = \frac{kA\Delta T}{L}$
Q - Heat Transfer Rate
Rate of heat transfer (W, kW, BTU/h)
k - Thermal Conductivity
Material thermal conductivity (W/(m·K))
A - Area
Cross-sectional area (m², cm², ft²)
ΔT - Temperature Difference
Temperature difference (K, °C, °F)
L - Thickness
Material thickness (m, cm, ft, in)
$Q = mc\Delta T$
Q - Heat Energy
Heat energy transferred (J, kJ, BTU, cal)
m - Mass
Mass of substance (kg, g, lb)
c - Specific Heat Capacity
Specific heat (J/(kg·K), cal/(g·°C))
ΔT - Temperature Change
Change in temperature (K, °C, °F)

Entropy & Second Law

$\Delta S = \int \frac{dQ}{T}$
ΔS - Change in Entropy
Entropy change (J/K, kJ/K)
Q - Heat
Heat transfer (J, kJ, BTU)
T - Temperature
Absolute temperature (K)

Efficiency

$\eta = \frac{W_{out}}{Q_{in}} = 1 - \frac{Q_{out}}{Q_{in}}$
η - Thermal Efficiency
Efficiency (dimensionless, 0-1)
W_out - Work Output
Useful work output (J, kJ, BTU)
Q_in - Heat Input
Heat energy input (J, kJ, BTU)
Q_out - Heat Rejected
Heat rejected to sink (J, kJ, BTU)
